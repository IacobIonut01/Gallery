**NFS** connects to exports from Unix-style servers and NAS boxes. Only **NFSv3 with AUTH_SYS** is supported; NFSv2 and NFSv4 are not. There is no password field: NFS trusts the user identity you present, so access is controlled by the export's rules on the server.

## Add an NFS export

1. Open **Settings › Cloud Accounts › Add NFS**.
2. **Server**: enter the address in **Server URL** as `nfs://host/export/path`, for example `nfs://192.168.1.20/export/photos`. Everything after the host is the exported path.
3. **Credentials**: optionally set the **User ID** as **uid:gid**, for example `1000:1000` — this is the numeric identity the export sees; ask the server's owner which uid:gid your account should present; a wrong one just means the export refuses or limits what you can touch. You can also set a **Display name** for the account here.
4. Tap **Test connection**; **Next** unlocks once it succeeds.
5. **Networking**, **Sync** (back up local folders), and **Review** work as on the other provider pages. Save to finish.

## Requirements

- The export must allow connections from the phone's IP address and offer an NFSv3 mount. Both are server-side settings; the app cannot change them, so ask the server's administrator if the test fails.
- The phone needs a local-network or VPN route to the server, and Android may prompt for **direct local network access**; grant it to proceed.

## Example: a NAS export

1. Your NAS exports `/export/photos` to your home network and your account there is `1000:1000`.
2. **Add NFS**, enter `nfs://192.168.1.20/export/photos`.
3. Set **User ID** to `1000:1000` and the display name `MyNAS NFS`, tap **Test connection**, then complete the wizard.
