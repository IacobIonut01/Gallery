**Extract frames** turns moments inside a video or a [motion photo](/ReFra/features/motion-photos) into ordinary photo files. Open the video or motion photo, tap **More options**, and choose **Extract frames**; it is offered only where a clip exists, not on still photos or GIFs.

## Pick your frames

<PreviewCollection feature="frame-extraction" section="pick" />

1. The **Choose frames** screen prepares the source first (**Preparing source…** with a progress percentage).
2. Step through the clip with **Previous frame** and **Next frame**, drag the filmstrip to seek, and use **Play preview** / **Pause preview** while you look.
3. **Add frame** marks the current frame; **Remove frame** unmarks it. Selected frames collect in a tray showing **Selected frames (N)** with a **Clear** option. At most **50 frames** per run; the app says so when you hit the limit.
4. With nothing selected, **Save current frame** exports just what is on screen; otherwise **Save N frames** exports the tray.

## Output format

<PreviewCollection feature="frame-extraction" section="format" />

The **Output format** choice is:

- **JPEG**: high quality (95), smaller files.
- **PNG**: lossless, larger files.

Frames are new files. If some could not carry date or location metadata, the result notes it ("Date or location metadata could not be written for N saved frames"), so check the saved photos' details if that matters.

## Saving and failures

- A clean run ends with **Saved N frames** and offers **View**, **Share**, or **Done**.
- A partial run reports **Saved N frames; M failed**; retry the failed ones.
- Cancelling asks first: **Cancel frame extraction?** Prepared data or unfinished export work is stopped.

## Example: a skateboard trick

1. Open the clip, **More options › Extract frames**.
2. Scrub to the takeoff, tap **Add frame**, then step forward and add the landing.
3. Choose **JPEG**, tap **Save 2 frames**, and open the results with **View**.
