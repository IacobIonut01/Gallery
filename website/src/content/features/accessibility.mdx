ReFra leans on Android's accessibility services and adds its own switches where they matter. These are the places to look; there is no single accessibility section; the options live where they act.

## Reading and contrast

- **Use system font** (Settings › Appearance) applies your configured font instead of the bundled one, and Android's font-size setting scales the interface.
- **Auto Contrast** and **Use Amoled Mode** in appearance tune readability; in the viewer, **Full brightness** helps outdoors. See [viewer settings](/ReFra/features/viewer-settings).

## Knowing what a control does

<PreviewCollection feature="accessibility" section="knowing-what-a-control-does" />

- **Display Selection Item's Title** (Settings › Navigation) puts text labels on the selection sheet's action icons, so actions are named rather than icon-only.
- **Use Material Navigation** shows labeled navigation on wide screens; **Right-align top actions** and **Fill action bar** in [selection actions](/ReFra/features/selection-actions) keep buttons from scrolling out of reach.

## Alternatives to gestures

<PreviewCollection feature="accessibility" section="alternatives-to-gestures" />

- Animations can be quieted with **Animate media items** off (see [appearance](/ReFra/features/appearance)).
- Where a gesture exists, a button usually does too: a video's fine seek has the seek bar, selection order has **Move up/down**, and the slideshow has visible **Previous**/**Next** controls.
- Settings screens are traversable with a keyboard or D-pad (focus moves between rows and Enter activates them) which is how the app is driven on TV-style devices.

## Example: set up for low vision

1. Enable **Use system font** and raise Android's font size.
2. Turn on **Display Selection Item's Title** so every action is labeled.
3. In the viewer, enable **Full brightness** for outdoor readability.
